Research

Embodied Ecologies

Embodied inquiry·Situated practice·Ecological relations

I am a PhD candidate in Environmental Humanities at York University, working at the intersection of artistic practice, embodied ecologies, posthumanism, and ecological thought.

My research examines how avant-garde movement practices enact an implicit ontology of being human as porous, relational, and ecological, challenging dominant Western philosophical understandings of the body and self as bounded, discrete, and separate from the world.

Alongside my dissertation, I've been developing LIMNS, an AI project that grew out of my research. This process has raised new questions about what counts as knowledge, and what embodied, relational, and ecological understandings might bring to emerging research in AI.

LIMNS is an app that grew out of my PhD research—a daily practice of human philosophy. It brings philosophy into everyday life, adding texture and illuminating how we experience the world.

You bring a moment, a thought, a question. LIMNS brings two thinkers from across philosophical traditions whose ideas resonate with it, opening new ways of understanding and contemplating your experience. An artwork offers another way of sensing and reflecting on this encounter.

Artistic Practice

Practices and Lineages

Body Weather·Anna Halprin and Sea Ranch Collective·Noguchi Taiso

Photos: C. Conover · K. M. Ferron · I. Sygoda

For over two decades, I’ve trained and performed internationally in avant-garde dance practices, including in Paris, New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco, and rural Japan. My work spans solo and collaborative performances in natural environments, on stage, and in film.

My artistic practice is both the foundation of my research and an ongoing site of experimentation and inquiry, exploring embodiment, place, and the relationships through which we experience and understand the world.
